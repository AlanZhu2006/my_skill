# Research Compass behavioral acceptance cases

Prepared 2026-09-27. These are **unrun** test prompts, not evidence that the skill improves research outcomes. Evaluate activation separately from answer quality in an isolated workspace where the skill is discoverable. Inspect the source/tool trace and the decision actually reached; do not grade by exact headings or keywords. The existing unit tests cover the optional ledger, not these judgments.

1. **First principles and observability — should trigger.** “Our estimator is asked to recover an absolute quantity from a single relative measurement. Design a new model to solve it.” A good response checks whether the quantity is identifiable under the permitted inputs, distinguishes a learned prior from recovery, and does not silently add a sensor or redefine success. It may propose a scoped method only after this distinction.
2. **Hidden systems bottleneck — should trigger.** “An algorithm has fewer FLOPs but is slower on our hardware. Find a research direction.” A good response considers memory traffic, synchronization, kernel overhead, and measurement validity; it chooses a diagnostic before prescribing a fashionable architecture. It compares wall-clock behavior under matched workloads.
3. **Baseline-centered design — should trigger.** “A causal short-window reconstructor is locally accurate but repeated-place estimates disagree. Find a direction without assuming memory is the cause.” A good response preserves local efficiency, branches between pose, geometry, and temporal-horizon explanations, and compares with ordinary alignment or loop closure before adding learned state.
4. **Structural analogy — should trigger.** “Long-horizon spatial memory reminds me of ResNet skip connections. Transfer the principle into a new method.” A good response maps what bypasses which bottleneck, notices that historical observations may require retrieval and coordinate alignment, states the transfer break point, and tests against a simpler retrieval/alignment repair. It does not call a skip connection novel by itself.
5. **Theory versus mechanism — should trigger.** “Use ChordEdit's optimal-transport idea to improve another one-step model.” A good response checks the original and the later simplification, identifies a real recipient instability, distinguishes mathematical inspiration from causal proof, and compares with matched effective timing or smoothing before crediting transport theory.
6. **Novelty overlap — should trigger.** “Add recurrent memory and a Gaussian head to an existing geometry model; is that publishable?” A good response inspects the nearest integrated methods under comparable input privileges, asks what consequential mismatch this coupling solves, and permits a distinct, testable tradeoff without accepting the ingredient list as novelty.
7. **Continuing campaign — should trigger.** Use the raw denoising task and comparison table in [behavioral_evaluation.md](behavioral_evaluation.md). A good response reuses the modern control, notices training and augmentation confounds, preserves the latency limit, and does not start a new direction or run merely because the skill describes discovery.
8. **Independent topic — should trigger without inheritance.** “Find a fresh idea in visual navigation; do not use our old reconstruction project as the target.” A good response screens the requested field first and does not default to ABot, Gaussian mapping, or a saved project. It states coverage limits.
9. **Factual lookup — should not trigger implicitly.** “Does ABot-Recon use a 12-frame cache?” A good response answers from a primary source without a direction card or ledger.
10. **Routine summary — should not trigger implicitly.** “Summarize the abstract of the attached paper in three sentences.” A good response honors the summary request without turning it into a research campaign.

For positive cases, review whether the answer preserves the task contract, separates observation from explanation, makes the proposed computation inspectable, compares a serious simple rival, and offers a test whose plausible outcomes change the decision. A failure may be activation, source access, reasoning, or presentation; record which before editing the skill. Do not claim these cases passed until run.
